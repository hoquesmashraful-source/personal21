# Figure visualisation style

## What the evidence shows

From the top 100 versus 163 matched controls (open access full texts), and a
coded review of 95 figures from 33 top papers:

* Top papers carry their findings in figures: median 5 figures versus 3;
  figures outnumber tables in 84% versus 58%.
* Maps appear in 55% of top papers versus 32%, and trends over time in 69%
  versus 30%. Highly cited papers lead with pictures of place and time.
* Figure captions are shorter in top papers (median 43 words versus 64) and
  explain every encoding.
* In 22 of 33 papers, figure 1 plotted a result showing one clear contrast.

Examples below are given as PMID and figure number.

## 2. Design style

### Layout

* Estimates papers favour multi-panel layouts, with a bold letter and short title at the top left of each panel.
* Small multiples hold axes fixed so the eye compares shapes. PMID 28408086 fig 1 keeps one relative risk axis across nine panels.
* PMID 28919117 fig 3 shares the y axis within each row. Rates fill the top row and counts the bottom, so flat rates sit above rising counts.
* A larger summary panel anchors a set. In PMID 29029897 fig 1 the World panel dwarfs the regional panels.

### Ordering

* By value: largest first (PMID 35065702 fig 2, PMID 33069327 fig 3).
* By geography: regions grouped within super-regions, with a grey global bar on top (PMID 30954305 fig 1).
* By time: rank charts run from the earliest year on the left (PMID 33069326 fig 2).
* By gradient: SDI quintiles or deprivation groups in order (PMID 30496104 fig 3, PMID 28641372 fig 1).
* Avoid alphabetical order. PMID 20668659 fig 3 lists dozens of studies by author name, so no pattern emerges.

### Colour

* Of 16 maps, 9 used diverging or spectral palettes, 5 sequential and 2 categorical. Most binned values into five to ten classes.
* Good sequential maps run yellow to red for mean BMI (PMID 27115820 fig 2) or yellow to blue for sepsis (PMID 31954465 fig 3).
* Paired maps share one colour scale, so years compare directly (PMID 33069326 fig 3, PMID 27115820 fig 2).
* Categorical colour encodes a hierarchy. With many causes, hue families keep groups readable: reds for communicable, blues for non-communicable and greens for injuries (PMID 38642570 fig 2, PMID 28919116 fig 3).
* Grey and black mark reference series, such as the global bar (PMID 30954305 fig 1) and the World panel (PMID 29029897 fig 1).
* Faded boxes mark causes that drop out of the top 25 (PMID 38642570 fig 1).
* Females were usually red or pink and males blue (PMID 38642570 fig 3, PMID 34998485 fig 1).
* Colour meanings did not carry across papers. Red meant non-communicable disease in PMID 33069326 fig 2 but infections in PMID 31954465 fig 2.

### Uncertainty

* 30 of 77 data figures showed uncertainty or significance. No map did.
* Trends and other curves used translucent bands (13 figures); overlapping bands stay readable in PMID 34634250 fig 1.
* Bars, dot plots and forest plots used interval lines (11 figures), as in PMID 35065702 fig 2.
* Dense rank charts print intervals in parentheses (PMID 38642570 fig 1) or bold significant changes (PMID 30496105 fig 2).
* Forecasts switch from solid to dashed lines while the band continues (PMID 34998485 fig 2).
* PMID 19299006 fig 2 sizes each square by its statistical information, so precise estimates dominate.

### Labelling

* Axis titles name the measure and unit, such as per 100 000 population, kg/m2 or %.
* Direct labels beat legends when series are few. PMID 28408086 fig 2 names each country at its line end and sets the global label in bold.
* Where legends appear, the best sit inside the plot in the order of the lines (PMID 18177778 fig 3, panel B).
* Ratios sit on log axes (PMID 32738937 fig 1). So do rates spanning orders of magnitude (PMID 38642570 fig 3).
* Axis breaks flag a truncated range (PMID 37356446 fig 2, PMID 19299006 fig 1).

### Annotation

* Reference marks give the benchmark: a dashed global mean and a green optimal band (PMID 30954305 fig 1), or BMI cut points at 25 and 30 (PMID 28604169 fig 3).
* PMID 30496103 fig 1 draws an identity line and prints a correlation coefficient in each panel.
* Event callouts turn an epidemic curve into a story (PMID 31995857 fig 1).
* Direction labels under a forest plot axis say which side favours what (PMID 32738937 fig 3).
* A black dot marks net change on decomposition bars (PMID 30496105 fig 3).

### Typography and density

* The Lancet house style dominates: one sans serif font, no background fill, no border and few gridlines.
* Density is high. Rank charts list 25 or more causes with intervals, and PMID 28919116 fig 1 prints a value in every country-year cell. Such figures double as citable reference tables.
* Some non-Lancet figures keep software defaults, such as grey backgrounds (PMID 21658213 fig 2).

### The first figure carries the headline

The strongest first figures show one contrast at a glance. PMID 33069326 fig 1 shows total burden flat while the age-standardised rate falls. PMID 31954465 fig 1 shows sepsis incidence falling, with bands. PMID 31995857 fig 1 marks control measures on the epidemic curve. When figure 1 is not a result, it usually shows the data behind the estimates (PMID 35065702 fig 1, PMID 30496104 fig 1).

## 3. Captions

### Length

* analysis.md reports a median of 43 words for top papers and 62 for controls.
* In this set the median is 45 words (interquartile range 22 to 79).
* Maps had a median of 29 words, time trends 38 and age curves 26. Forest plots (80) and rank change charts (73) needed more.

### Structure

Lancet captions have two parts. The first is a title phrase with no full stop: measure and unit, population, place, grouping and years. The second is a run of short notes. These say what lines, bands or bars mean, add method notes, point to the appendix and list abbreviations alphabetically. NEJM and BMJ captions often open with a title sentence, then describe each panel.

### What they include

* A year or period appeared in 67% of captions and in all 16 map captions.
* 10 of 16 map captions said the measure was age-standardised.
* 51% defined abbreviations, including 12 of 16 map captions.
* Of 26 figures that drew intervals, 17 captions named the interval. The rest put it in an axis or column header.
* 15% pointed to an appendix or web table. Data sources were rarely named (an exception is PMID 18177778 fig 1).

### Examples (paraphrased)

* Trend (PMID 31954465 fig 1, 22 words): sepsis incidence per 100 000, age-standardised, by cause, 1990 to 2017. A note says shaded areas are 95% uncertainty intervals.
* Map (PMID 32061315 fig 1, 29 words): DALY rate for chronic kidney disease, 2017, then abbreviations for small states.
* Ranked bar (PMID 35065702 fig 2, 44 words): death rate by region, 2019, then how drugs were aggregated and what error bars show.
* Panels (PMID 28641372 fig 1, 62 words): one sentence names panels A to D, then defines deprivation cut points and cites a web table for counts.

Weak captions say almost nothing. "Flow diagram." (PMID 20668659 fig 1) gives no dates or totals.

### Caption template

> [Measure] ([unit]) [among population], by [grouping], [place], [first year] to [last year]. Lines show [estimate type]; shaded areas show 95% [uncertainty or confidence] intervals. [Method note.] [Numbers in appendix p X.] [ABC=full term.]

Aim for 25 to 50 words.

## 5. Practical recipes

The sketches were reviewed by hand, not run; check them on your data. All share one theme. Replace data frames and column names with your own.

```r
library(ggplot2)
theme_pub <- theme_classic(base_size = 9) +
  theme(strip.background = element_blank(),
        strip.text = element_text(face = "bold", hjust = 0),
        legend.key.size = unit(3, "mm"))
```

### 5.1 Trend lines with uncertainty bands

* Data: group, year, estimate, lower and upper bounds.
* Encoding: line for the estimate, band at 20 to 30% opacity, dashed line for forecasts.
* Order and colour: up to five hues per panel, labelled at the line end; black for the global line.
* Caption: "[Measure] per 100 000, by [group], [years]. Shaded areas show 95% uncertainty intervals."

```r
# d: group, year, est, lo, hi, fc (TRUE for forecast years)
# repeat the last observed year in the forecast rows so lines join
ggplot(d, aes(year, est, colour = group, fill = group)) +
  geom_ribbon(aes(ymin = lo, ymax = hi), alpha = 0.25, colour = NA) +
  geom_line(aes(linetype = fc), linewidth = 0.7) +
  geom_text(data = subset(d, year == max(year)), aes(label = group),
            hjust = 0, nudge_x = 0.5, size = 3) +
  scale_linetype_manual(values = c("solid", "dashed")) +
  scale_x_continuous(expand = expansion(mult = c(0, 0.2))) +
  labs(x = "Year", y = "Age-standardised rate per 100 000") +
  theme_pub + theme(legend.position = "none")
```

To show rates falling while counts rise, use two panels with one x axis, not a second y axis.

### 5.2 Choropleth map

* Data: one value per country or district, joined to boundaries by ISO code.
* Encoding: fill in five to eight classes with round breaks; grey for no data.
* Colour: sequential for rates; diverging only around a true midpoint such as zero change.
* Layout: one panel per year or sex, one shared legend, insets for small states.
* Uncertainty: give intervals in an appendix table or a companion ranked chart.
* Caption: "[Age-standardised measure] ([unit]) by country, [year]. Grey shows no data. [Inset abbreviations.]"

```r
library(sf)
# world: sf polygons with iso3; est: iso3, year, rate
m <- merge(world, est, by = "iso3", all.x = TRUE)
ggplot(m) +
  geom_sf(aes(fill = rate), colour = "white", linewidth = 0.1) +
  scale_fill_fermenter(palette = "YlOrRd", direction = 1,
                       breaks = c(100, 200, 400, 800, 1600),
                       na.value = "grey80", name = "Rate per\n100 000") +
  facet_wrap(~ year, ncol = 1) +   # one shared scale for all years
  coord_sf(crs = "+proj=robin") +
  theme_void(base_size = 9) + theme(legend.position = "left")
# change from baseline: palette = "RdBu", limits = c(-50, 50)
```

### 5.3 Ranked bar or dot chart with intervals

* Data: region or cause, estimate and bounds.
* Encoding and order: horizontal dots or bars, largest at top; bars must start at zero.
* Colour: grey, or one hue per super-region.
* Caption: "[Measure] ([unit]) by [region], [year]. Lines show 95% uncertainty intervals."

```r
d$region <- reorder(d$region, d$est)   # largest at top
ggplot(d, aes(est, region, colour = superregion)) +
  geom_linerange(aes(xmin = lo, xmax = hi), linewidth = 0.6) +
  geom_point(size = 2) +
  labs(x = "Deaths per 100 000 population", y = NULL, colour = NULL) +
  theme_pub + theme(legend.position = "top")
# bars: geom_col(fill = "grey60") + geom_errorbar(aes(xmin = lo, xmax = hi), width = 0.3)
```

### 5.4 Rank change chart

* Data: cause, group, rank in the first and last year.
* Encoding: two columns of labelled boxes joined by lines; dashed when rank falls.
* Colour: pale fill by cause group; faded fill for causes leaving the list.
* Uncertainty: intervals printed beside values, or bold for significant change.
* Caption: "Leading [N] causes of [measure], [year A] and [year B]. Solid lines show a rise or no change in rank; dashed lines show a fall."

```r
# r: cause, group, rank_a, rank_b
ggplot(r) +
  geom_segment(aes(x = 1, xend = 2, y = rank_a, yend = rank_b,
                   linetype = rank_b > rank_a), colour = "grey40") +
  geom_label(aes(1, rank_a, label = paste(rank_a, cause), fill = group),
             hjust = 1, size = 2.5) +
  geom_label(aes(2, rank_b, label = paste(rank_b, cause), fill = group),
             hjust = 0, size = 2.5) +
  scale_y_reverse() +
  scale_linetype_manual(values = c("solid", "dashed"), guide = "none") +
  scale_fill_manual(values = c("#F4CCCC", "#CFE2F3", "#D9EAD3")) +
  xlim(-0.5, 3.5) + theme_void(base_size = 9)
```

### 5.5 Small multiples of age or exposure curves

* Data: age or exposure level, sex, panel variable, estimate and bounds.
* Encoding: one line and band per sex; panels ordered along a gradient such as SDI.
* Colour: two sex hues, identical in every figure.
* Caption: "[Measure] by age and sex, by [panel variable], [year]. Log scale. Shaded areas show 95% uncertainty intervals."

```r
d$sdi <- factor(d$sdi, levels = c("Low", "Low-middle", "Middle",
                                  "High-middle", "High"))
ggplot(d, aes(age, est, colour = sex, fill = sex)) +
  geom_ribbon(aes(ymin = lo, ymax = hi), alpha = 0.2, colour = NA) +
  geom_line() +
  facet_grid(cause ~ sdi) +   # scales = "free_y" frees whole rows only
  scale_y_log10() +
  scale_colour_manual(values = c(Female = "#C0392B", Male = "#2471A3"),
                      aesthetics = c("colour", "fill")) +
  labs(x = "Age (years)", y = "DALYs per 100 000 (log scale)",
       colour = NULL, fill = NULL) +
  theme_pub
```

### 5.6 Stacked composition and decomposition bars

* Data: counts by age and cause; or signed change by driver, plus net change.
* Encoding: stacked bars; decomposition bars split at zero with a dot for net change.
* Colour: hue families by cause group, light to dark within each family.
* Caption: "Number of [outcome] by age and cause, [years]. Bar height shows the total."

```r
# d: age, cause (factor ordered by group), deaths; pal: named colours
ggplot(d, aes(age, deaths / 1e6, fill = cause)) +
  geom_col(width = 0.8) +
  facet_wrap(~ sex, ncol = 1) +
  scale_fill_manual(values = pal) +
  labs(x = "Age group", y = "Deaths (millions)", fill = NULL) +
  theme_pub

# dc: outcome, driver, change (signed); net: outcome, change
ggplot(dc, aes(change, outcome, fill = driver)) +
  geom_col(width = 0.6) +
  geom_vline(xintercept = 0, linetype = "dashed") +
  geom_point(data = net, aes(change, outcome), inherit.aes = FALSE) +
  labs(x = "Percentage change", y = NULL, fill = NULL) +
  theme_pub
```

### 5.7 Forest plot

* Data: study or subgroup, estimate, bounds and weight.
* Encoding: square sized by weight, interval line, vertical line at 1, log axis.
* Order: by group, then year or effect size, never alphabetical by default.
* Caption: "[Ratio] (95% CI) for [exposure] and [outcome], by [subgroup]. Squares are sized by weight."

```r
d$label <- factor(d$label, levels = rev(d$label))   # keep grouped order
ggplot(d, aes(est, label)) +
  geom_vline(xintercept = 1, colour = "grey50") +
  geom_linerange(aes(xmin = lo, xmax = hi)) +
  geom_point(aes(size = w), shape = 15) +
  geom_text(aes(x = 6, label = sprintf("%.2f (%.2f to %.2f)", est, lo, hi)),
            hjust = 0, size = 2.6) +
  scale_x_log10(breaks = c(0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4)) +
  scale_size_area(max_size = 3, guide = "none") +
  coord_cartesian(xlim = c(0.25, 4), clip = "off") +
  labs(x = "Hazard ratio (95% CI)", y = NULL) +
  theme_pub + theme(plot.margin = margin(5, 80, 5, 5))
```

### 5.8 Heat map of values or data coverage

* Data: place, year and a value or count of sources.
* Encoding: tiles with printed values; places grouped by region; white for no data.
* Colour: sequential.
* Caption: "[Measure] by [place] and year, [years]. White cells show no data."

```r
ggplot(d, aes(year, place, fill = value)) +
  geom_tile(colour = "white") +
  geom_text(aes(label = round(value)), size = 2) +
  scale_fill_distiller(palette = "YlOrRd", direction = 1, na.value = "white") +
  scale_y_discrete(limits = rev) +
  scale_x_continuous(position = "top", expand = c(0, 0)) +
  labs(x = NULL, y = NULL, fill = "Value (%)") +
  theme_minimal(base_size = 7) + theme(panel.grid = element_blank())
# coverage version: geom_point(aes(size = n_sources)) instead of tiles
```

## 6. Common weaknesses to avoid

* Diverging palettes on one-sided data. Rates (PMID 32061315 fig 1) and an all-positive change (PMID 34998485 fig 3) sit on blue to red scales, implying a false midpoint.
* Too many classes or colours: 19 map classes in PMID 34998485 fig 3, 18 stacked cause colours in PMID 33069327 fig 3, and 22 marked series in PMID 32061315 fig 3.
* Dual y axes. In PMID 33069326 fig 1 any crossing is an artefact of scaling; paired panels (PMID 34998485 fig 2) avoid this.
* Silent scale changes. PMID 33069327 fig 1 alters both axis ranges across six panels.
* Ratios on linear axes, which stretch increases and squeeze decreases (PMID 30002074 fig 3).
* Missing intervals where data allow them, as in the bars of PMID 28641372 fig 2.
* Software defaults: grey backgrounds and no axis titles (PMID 21658213 fig 2), raw variable names and decimal proportions (PMID 28604169 fig 1).
* Meaning hidden in the caption: PMID 21658213 fig 2 has no legend, and PMID 28641372 fig 2 has no panel titles.
* Number dumps. PMID 20668659 fig 3 prints Z and P values to three decimals beside an alphabetical study list.
* Empty captions and low resolution diagrams (PMID 32112714 fig 1, PMID 22276600 fig 1).

## 7. Checklist

* [ ] Figure 1 shows the main finding as one clear contrast.
* [ ] Result figures show place, time or both where data allow.
* [ ] Each figure answers one message from the decision table.
* [ ] Order follows value, geography, time or gradient.
* [ ] Small multiples share axes, or the caption says they do not.
* [ ] Sequential palettes show magnitude; diverging palettes need a true midpoint.
* [ ] Maps use five to eight classes, one legend, grey for no data and insets.
* [ ] One colour mapping holds across the paper; the global series is black or grey.
* [ ] Every estimate with an interval shows it; forecasts are dashed.
* [ ] Ratios use a log axis with a line at 1.
* [ ] Axis titles give measure and unit; lines are labelled directly when few.
* [ ] The caption opens with measure, unit, population, place and years.
* [ ] The caption explains encodings and abbreviations in 25 to 50 words.
* [ ] Text is legible at print size; files are vector or high resolution.
