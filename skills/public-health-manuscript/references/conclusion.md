# Conclusion

## What the corpus shows

* 72% of papers have a separate Conclusion. Median 134 words (IQR 100 to
  197), usually one paragraph.
* 91% give a policy or programme recommendation; 67% restate the main
  finding; 32% repeat numbers; 17% use causal language; 14% call for
  further research.
* Lancet-family papers usually fold the conclusion into the last Discussion
  paragraph; only 2 of 20 top-journal exemplars had a separate Conclusion
  section, against 13 of 20 others. JAMA uses a two-sentence Conclusions
  section. Follow the target journal.

## How to write it

Three to six sentences:

1. The answer to the research question, in plain words, without statistics.
2. What it means for the population or the field.
3. One or two recommendations matched to the design.
4. The key research or data gap, if there is one.

**Match the recommendation to the design.**

| Finding type | Supported recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Descriptive levels, trends, inequalities | Monitoring, targeting, prioritising groups or areas |
| Associations from cross-sectional data | Further evaluation (cohort, trial, implementation study); attention to a group; hypotheses for programmes |
| Measurement or methods findings | Advice on indicators, questions or analysis methods |

Recommendations that outrun the data are the most common weakness: urging
specific programmes, curricula or national policies that the study did not
evaluate, or listing countries for "urgent action" from associations alone.

## Sentence frames

1. "In [setting], [main finding in plain words]."
2. "These findings support [monitoring or targeting action] for [group] and
   call for [design] to test whether [intervention] improves [outcome]."
3. "[Indicator] should be tracked by [wealth, region or another dimension]
   so that progress among [group] is visible."
4. "Future surveys could add [question or module] to measure [aspect] more
   directly."

## Checklist

* [ ] Three to six sentences; about 100 to 200 words (or the journal's form)
* [ ] Answer restated without repeating statistics
* [ ] Recommendations match the design; no causal claims from associations
* [ ] No claim that is absent from the Results
* [ ] Names the key research or data gap, if any
