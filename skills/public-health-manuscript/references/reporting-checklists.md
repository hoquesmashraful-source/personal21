# Reporting checklists

Use STROBE for cross-sectional analyses of DHS and MICS. Add RECORD items when
the data were routinely collected, GATHER when the paper produces population
health estimates (prevalence, trends, burden), and TRIPOD for prediction models.
Only 12% of the highly cited corpus cited a guideline, but 27% of papers in top
journals did, and most top journals now require a completed checklist.

## STROBE for cross-sectional studies, mapped to manuscript sections

| Section | Item | What to write |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title and abstract | 1 | Design in the title or abstract; an informative, balanced summary |
| Introduction | 2 | Scientific background and rationale |
| Introduction | 3 | Specific objectives, including any prespecified hypotheses |
| Methods | 4 | Key elements of study design, early in the paper |
| Methods | 5 | Setting, locations, dates of data collection |
| Methods | 6 | Eligibility criteria and sources and methods of selecting participants |
| Methods | 7 | Define all outcomes, exposures, predictors, confounders and effect modifiers |
| Methods | 8 | Data sources and measurement for each variable |
| Methods | 9 | Efforts to address potential sources of bias |
| Methods | 10 | How the study size was arrived at |
| Methods | 11 | How quantitative variables were handled and why groupings were chosen |
| Methods | 12a | All statistical methods, including those used to control for confounding |
| Methods | 12b | Methods for subgroups and interactions |
| Methods | 12c | How missing data were addressed |
| Methods | 12d | Analytic methods taking account of the sampling strategy |
| Methods | 12e | Sensitivity analyses |
| Results | 13 | Numbers at each stage of selection; reasons for exclusion; flow diagram |
| Results | 14 | Characteristics of participants; number with missing data per variable |
| Results | 15 | Numbers of outcome events or summary measures |
| Results | 16a | Unadjusted and adjusted estimates with precision (95% CI); which confounders and why |
| Results | 16b | Category boundaries for categorised continuous variables |
| Results | 16c | Absolute risk where relevant |
| Results | 17 | Other analyses (subgroups, interactions, sensitivity) |
| Discussion | 18 | Key results with reference to objectives |
| Discussion | 19 | Limitations, including direction and magnitude of potential bias |
| Discussion | 20 | Cautious overall interpretation, considering objectives, limitations, multiplicity and other evidence |
| Discussion | 21 | Generalisability (external validity) |
| Other | 22 | Funding and the role of funders |

Items most often missed in the corpus: 12c missing data (17% addressed it),
12e sensitivity analyses (8%), 13 sample flow (flow charts in 9%), and 19
direction of bias.

## RECORD additions (routinely collected data)

* Name the database (survey, round, recode version) and how it was accessed.
* Describe the codes and algorithms used to define variables.
* Describe any data linkage (for example, GPS clusters to facility data).
* State the data cleaning methods.

## GATHER (global health estimates)

Use when the main output is estimates such as national prevalence, trends or
subnational maps. Key items: list all data inputs with years and sample sizes;
describe how inputs were adjusted or excluded; describe the model with enough
detail to reproduce it; give uncertainty intervals; provide code and estimates
as supplementary files.

## Other reporting guidance

* SAMPL: basic statistical reporting (report estimates with CIs; give exact
  p values; avoid "p = 0.000").
* TRIPOD (or TRIPOD+AI): prediction or machine-learning models.
* CONSORT and PRISMA do not apply to survey analyses unless the paper is a
  trial or a systematic review.
