# Results writing

Figure design is in `figures.md`.

## What the evidence shows

* Results are long in top papers (median 2,816 words versus 1,207 in
  controls), but the numbers live in figures: median 5 figures and 2 tables
  versus 3 and 2. Figures outnumber tables in 84% of top papers versus 58%.
* Trend figures (69% versus 30%) and maps (55% versus 32%) are far more
  common.
* Every paragraph points to a figure, table or appendix page.

## Order: from the whole to the parts

Total, then broad groups, then specific causes or subgroups, then age and sex,
then regions, then countries. Trends and attributable burden follow levels.
Some papers open by describing the data (number of sources, participants).

## The first Results sentence (four patterns)

1. **Headline estimate with interval and definition.** The antimicrobial
   resistance paper opens with 1.27 million deaths (95% UI 0.911 to 1.71)
   attributable to resistance, naming the counterfactual (rank 3, PMID
   35065702).
2. **Counts versus rates.** GBD 2019 opens by noting flat DALY counts but
   improving age-standardised rates (rank 1, PMID 33069326).
3. **Composition.** The share of deaths in each broad cause group (rank 24,
   PMID 30496103).
4. **Sample first.** The number of people measured (rank 25, PMID 24570244).

## How numbers are reported

* Standard unit: a count with its interval, then a rate, then a percentage
  change with its interval.
* Write "95% UI" or "95% CI" once, then bare brackets if the journal allows.
  In drafts, use "to" between limits, not a dash.
* Name both extremes with places and values. Count units past a threshold
  ("79 of 195 countries"). State concentration ("almost a third of cases in
  two countries").
* Give counts and age-standardised rates side by side; they can move in
  opposite directions.
* Report annualised change and compare periods (for example 1990 to 2010
  against 2010 to 2019). Group trends with a stated cut-off.
* Say when intervals overlap, and warn when a pattern may be an artefact
  (for example reporting delay in outbreak data, rank 7, PMID 31995857).
* Keep p values in the text, if at all; tables and figures carry estimates
  and intervals.

## Text versus figures

Text interprets the pattern; figures and tables carry the detail. Do not
restate every number from a table. One sentence per message, then a pointer.

## Sentence frames

1. "In [year], an estimated [count] ([lower] to [upper]) people had
   [outcome], an age-standardised rate of [rate] ([lower] to [upper]) per
   100 000."
2. "From [year] to [year], the number of [outcome] rose by [x]% ([lower] to
   [upper]), while the age-standardised rate changed by [y]% ([lower] to
   [upper])."
3. "Rates ranged from [value] ([lower] to [upper]) in [lowest] to [value]
   ([lower] to [upper]) in [highest] (figure [n])."
4. "[Number] of [total] [units] exceeded [threshold], and [number] [units]
   held [x]% of all [cases]."
5. "Compared with [counterfactual], [exposure] accounted for [count]
   ([lower] to [upper]) [outcomes], or [x]% ([lower] to [upper]) of the
   total."
6. "[Outcome] was [x] percentage points higher in [group A] than in [group
   B] ([value] vs [value]; 95% CI [lower] to [upper])."
7. "Under [sensitivity analysis], the estimate moved from [main value] to
   [new value] ([lower] to [upper]); the ranking of [groups] held."

## Weaknesses to avoid

* P values without absolute numbers or changes.
* Text that repeats tables; too few figures for trends and places.
* Ranking groups whose intervals overlap without saying so.
* Counts without rates, or rates without counts.
* Sensitivity results described in words but not numbers.

## Checklist

* [ ] First sentence gives the headline number with its interval, or the
      sample
* [ ] Order runs from overall to subgroups to places
* [ ] Counts, rates and percentage changes each carry an interval
* [ ] Extremes, thresholds and concentration stated with values
* [ ] Every paragraph points to a figure, table or appendix page
* [ ] Trends and places shown in figures
* [ ] Overlapping intervals and possible artefacts flagged
* [ ] Sensitivity results reported with numbers
