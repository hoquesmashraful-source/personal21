Study: trends in child stunting by household wealth in 30 sub-Saharan African countries, using 87 DHS surveys (2000 to 2021), children under 5 (n=612,408).

Main results (from my Stata output):
- Pooled stunting prevalence fell from 41.6% (95% CI 40.9 to 42.3) in 2000-04 to 32.1% (31.6 to 32.6) in 2017-21.
- Annual absolute change varied by country: largest decline in Ethiopia (-1.3 percentage points per year, 95% CI -1.5 to -1.1), no change in Madagascar (0.0, -0.3 to 0.3), increase in Zambia? no, Zambia declined (-0.9, -1.1 to -0.7).
- Gap between poorest and richest quintile (slope index of inequality): 21.4 percentage points in 2000-04 (95% CI 19.8 to 23.0) and 19.9 (18.7 to 21.1) in 2017-21. Gap narrowed in 11 countries, widened in 6, no clear change in 13.
- I have country-level estimates for each survey and each wealth quintile, with CIs.
Target journal: Lancet Global Health.
