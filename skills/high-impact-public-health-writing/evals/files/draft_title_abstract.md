# Title
A study of hypertension in some Asian countries

# Abstract
Hypertension is a very important public health problem all over the world and it is increasing day by day in many countries, especially in low-income and middle-income countries where health systems are weak and people do not know about their blood pressure. There are few studies on this topic. The aim of this study was to see the situation of hypertension. We used data from WHO STEPS surveys done in Bangladesh (2018), Bhutan (2019), Nepal (2019), Sri Lanka (2021), Myanmar (2014), Mongolia (2019) and Timor-Leste (2014), which included 38,412 adults aged 18 to 69 years. Hypertension was defined as systolic BP 140 or more, diastolic BP 90 or more, or taking medication. We did logistic regression. The pooled age-standardised prevalence was 27.4% (95% CI 26.6 to 28.2). It was highest in Mongolia (33.9%, 95% CI 32.1 to 35.7) and lowest in Timor-Leste (17.8%, 95% CI 16.2 to 19.5). Among people with hypertension, 41.2% were aware, 26.5% were treated and 9.8% were controlled. Men had higher odds of uncontrolled hypertension (aOR 1.62, 95% CI 1.41 to 1.86). Older age and obesity were also significant. In conclusion hypertension is very high and governments must take urgent action to solve this problem.
